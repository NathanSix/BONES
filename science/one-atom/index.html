    Why it took a long exposure

    The ion throws its light in every direction, and the lens catches only a sliver. Stopping the lens down so both needle tips stay sharp cuts that sliver further. This simulates the sensor pixels right around the ion. The settings are estimates you can change, not the exact ones used for the photo.

    Turning up the laser stops helping past a point. The ion can't absorb a new photon until it has re-emitted the last one, which caps it near 68 million photons per second.
