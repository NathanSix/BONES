Why left and right molecules matter, told with hands, keys, and germs. Blue always means left. Pink always means right.

Page 1

Your two hands are twins that don't match

Same fingers. Same thumb. Mirror images.

Stack them and turn them any way you like. The thumbs never line up. Flipping a hand over doesn't help either, because then you'd see the back of the hand, not the palm.

Page 2

The mirror test

Hold a thing up to a mirror. If the mirror picture is exactly the same thing, it has no hand. If the mirror picture is a different version, it is handed.

BallMirror is the same. No hand.
MugJust turn it around. No hand.
HandMirror is a different hand. Handed!

Scientists have a big word for "handed": chiral (say it "KY-rull"). It comes from the Greek word for hand. Chirality just means handedness. That's all it is.

Tiny molecules have hands too

Many of the building blocks of living things come in a left version and a right version. Same atoms, mirror shapes, just like your hands. And life follows two strict rules:

Proteins use LEFT

Proteins are chains of amino acids. Every one is a left-handed piece.

DNA sugars use RIGHT

The sugar in the backbone of DNA and RNA is the right-handed version.

Space makes both hands about equally. Life picked one hand for each job and never switched. Every living thing has the same picks, which is a big clue we all come from one shared ancestor. Why left? Nobody knows yet.

Page 4

Shape is everything: the lock and key

Your body is full of tiny machines that grab molecules. Each one works like a lock. The molecule is the key. Pick a key and try it.

The pins in the lock are shaped for the normal key.

The mirror key is made of the same metal with the same bumps, just flipped. It still won't work. Your body's locks were all built for life's hands, so a mirror molecule can't be grabbed, read, or cut up.

Why a mirror germ would be scary

Your immune system is a team of guards. They catch germs by grabbing their shapes. Watch what happens with a normal germ, then a mirror germ.

Caught 0 Still loose 0

Normal germs get grabbed and cleaned up.

A mirror germ slips past every guard, because none of their grabbers fit it. Nothing breaks it down, so it keeps making copies. That is why scientists warned in 2024 that nobody should build a mirror cell at all.
