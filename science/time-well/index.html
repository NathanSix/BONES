Gravitational time dilation

The Time Well

Mass slows down time near it. The closer you sit to Earth's center, the slower your clock ticks. Moving fast slows it down too. These effects are real, and atomic clocks have measured them. They're just very, very small.

The rules

Two things slow a clock

Gravity

Closer to a big mass, time runs slower. Climb higher and it runs faster.

Speed

The faster you move, the slower your clock ticks. This is the astronaut effect.

Your head is older than your feet

Stand up and your head sits about 1.7 m above your feet. Over an 80-year life, your head ends up about half a millionth of a second older.

2010 · NIST

Physicists raised one aluminum-ion clock about a third of a meter above another. The higher one ran faster, by roughly 90 billionths of a second over a 79-year life.

2022 · JILA

A single cloud of atoms used as a clock. Its top ticked faster than its bottom, across a height of about one millimeter.

The equator trap

The equator bulges, so a clock there sits about 21 km farther from Earth's center than a clock at a pole. That alone would make it run faster.

But the equator also spins at about 1,040 mph, and that speed slows it by the same amount.

SPIN SPEED−104 ns BULGE HEIGHT+104 ns
▲ net per day: zero, a tie

It cancels exactly because Earth's spin is what pushed the bulge out in the first place. The oceans settled where gravity's pull and the spin's push balance, and that same balance makes every sea-level clock on Earth agree.

Why things fall

Throw a ball so it lands back in your hand 2 seconds later. Now imagine forcing it along other arcs that also take 2 seconds. Higher arcs spend time up where clocks run faster, but they need more speed, and speed slows the ball's clock.

as = attosecond, a billionth of a billionth of a second

The winning arc is exactly the one a real ball follows. Physicists call this the principle of maximal aging: a falling object takes the path that gives its own clock the most time. For slow, everyday things, most of what we feel as gravity is this stretching of time.

The short version

Say it to a kid

  • Heavy things slow down time near them. Earth slows our clocks down a tiny bit.
  • Go higher and time speeds up. Your head is a tiny bit older than your feet.
  • Moving fast slows your clock too. Astronauts come home a tiny bit younger than us.
  • GPS satellites fly so high that gravity wins. Their clocks run fast, and engineers set them to correct for it.
  • Things fall because time runs at different speeds at different heights.
